## Location Overview

The *Brass Phoenix* is a triumph of industrial engineering and luxury design—a 400-foot dirigible that represents the pinnacle of Meridian Airship Lines' ambitions. Her hull gleams with polished brass plating, and her twin steam engines can maintain cruising altitude of 10,000 feet for weeks without refueling.

Built over two years at the Coalport Naval Yards, the Phoenix incorporates cutting-edge innovations: triple-redundant ballast systems, a revolutionary chronometer-based navigation array, and passenger accommodations that rival the finest hotels.

This is her maiden voyage—Coalport to Meridian Station, carrying wealthy passengers, critical cargo, and the reputation of everyone involved in her construction.

The ship's design reflects steampunk Victorian aesthetics: brass and mahogany interiors, crystal chandeliers that sway with the airship's motion, observation windows offering panoramic sky views. Passengers can pretend they're floating through clouds in a gentleman's club, insulated from the industrial reality of coal furnaces and steam pistons that actually keep them aloft.

But beneath the luxury facade, the Phoenix is a working vessel with crew quarters, machine shops, cargo holds, and engine rooms where the real work happens—and where saboteurs can operate unnoticed.

## Current State Narrative

The *Brass Phoenix* cruises at 8,000 feet, six hours from Meridian Station. The sabotaged chronometer has been repaired, but the damage to crew morale and passenger confidence lingers.

The approaching Titanstorm dominates the forward horizon—a wall of dark clouds threaded with lightning, churning the atmosphere into chaos. Navigation through such storms requires precision timing and accurate altitude measurements. Without the chronometer, attempting passage would be suicide.

The crew is on edge. Word of the sabotage has spread through below-deck gossip. Passengers alternate between fear (someone tried to destroy the ship) and indignation (how dare someone endanger their journey).

Captain Ironwood maintains public calm through force of personality, but privately he's furious. Someone aboard his ship—crew or passenger—orchestrated an act of sabotage that could have killed everyone aboard.

The investigation continues. The chronometer is repaired, but the saboteur is still at large. And the storm window is closing.

Three hours until storm boundary. The Phoenix must navigate the Titanstorm—or turn back and abort the maiden voyage in failure.

## Notable Features

### The Navigation Deck (Bridge)

The ship's nerve center, located in the forward hull behind massive observation windows. Houses the chronometer, navigation charts, altitude controls, and communication systems.

**Current Status:** Restricted access after sabotage. Only authorized personnel allowed.

**Equipment:**
- Primary Chronometer (recently repaired)
- Backup Chronometer (manual wind, less precise)
- Navigation Charts (Titanstorm region)
- Altitude Pressure Gauges
- Aethergram Communications System
- Ship's Whistle Controls

**Security:** Door requires officer's key. Captain Ironwood and Chief Navigator Castellan have access. Chief Engineer Graylock had override access (how he sabotaged the chronometer).

---

### The Machine Shop

Located on the engineering deck, this workshop contains lathes, welding equipment, brass-working tools, and everything needed for in-flight repairs.

**Current Status:** Under investigation after brass shavings linked to the chronometer sabotage were found.

**Equipment:**
- Precision lathe (used to create sabotage shim)
- Welding station
- Tool storage (organized by Chief Engineer Graylock)
- Brass stock and materials
- Work benches

**Security:** Supposed to be locked after hours, but crew often leaves it accessible for emergency repairs.

**Notable:** Operation log shows lathe was used at 02:47 on the night of sabotage.

---

### The Passenger Saloon

A magnificent space of polished mahogany and brass, featuring leather seating, a small bar, dining tables, and observation windows running the full length of both sides.

**Current Status:** Open, serving as social hub and gossip central.

**Amenities:**
- Dining area (seats 20)
- Reading nook with technical journals and adventure novels
- Bar (limited alcohol selection)
- Observation windows (180-degree views)
- Card tables (site of Lord Ashworth's gambling)

**Atmosphere:** Tense. Passengers are aware something is wrong but lack full details. Rumors spread faster than truth.

**Notable Passengers:**
- Lord Ashworth (gambling debts, nervous)
- Cordelia Vex (suspected spy, watching everything)
- Professor Minerva Cogsworth (inventor, suspiciously knowledgeable)
- Various merchants and minor nobles

---

### The Cargo Hold

A cavernous lower-deck space containing passengers' luggage, commercial freight, and the mysterious "special shipment" sealed with Meridian Station official marks.

**Current Status:** Mostly locked, accessed only by cargo master and captain.

**Contents:**
- Passenger luggage (standard trunks and cases)
- Commercial freight (textiles, machine parts, luxury goods)
- **"Special Shipment"** (4 large crates, Meridian Station seals)
- Ventilation shafts (where regulator crystal was hidden)

**Security:** Main doors locked. Ventilation access points scattered throughout. Lord Ashworth admitted to gambling here after-hours (suggesting security isn't perfect).

**Mystery:** The "special shipment" is never mentioned to passengers, and crew have orders not to discuss it.

---

### The Engine Room

The beating heart of the Phoenix—two massive steam engines, coal furnaces, ballast tanks, and the pressure systems that keep the airship aloft.

**Current Status:** Fully operational, running at 75% capacity (efficient cruising speed).

**Features:**
- Twin steam engines (each capable of independent operation)
- Coal furnaces (stoked every 2 hours)
- Ballast water tanks (altitude control)
- Emergency venting systems
- Pressure gauges everywhere

**Crew:** Chief Engineer Graylock (currently under suspicion), 4 assistant engineers, 6 coal stokers

**Atmosphere:** Hot, loud, perpetually busy. This is where working-class crew actually keep the ship flying while passengers sip tea above.

## Historical Context

The *Brass Phoenix* represents a decade of technological advancement in airship design. Previous luxury dirigibles could cruise at 6,000 feet maximum. The Phoenix's advanced engines and hull design allow 10,000-foot operations—above most weather patterns.

Her chronometer-based navigation system is revolutionary. Traditional dirigibles navigate by landmarks and compass—unreliable in clouds or storms. The Phoenix's chronometer array allows dead-reckoning navigation with unprecedented accuracy.

Captain Ironwood insisted on the chronometer system despite additional cost. His experience navigating the Borderlands Conflict taught him that precision matters when lives are at stake.

The ship's name references both rebirth (Phoenix from ashes) and her brass construction. Meridian Airship Lines chose it deliberately—this vessel is meant to resurrect luxury air travel after three years of economic decline.

But names carry expectations. A Phoenix must rise triumphant. A Phoenix must survive its trials.

The sabotage threatens to burn the Phoenix's reputation before she ever reaches her destination.

## The Storm

The Titanstorm dominates the forward view—a churning mass of dark clouds stretching from 4,000 to 18,000 feet. Lightning flickers within like veins of electricity.

Navigating the storm requires:
- Precise altitude control (must stay between 8,000-9,000 feet)
- Accurate timing (passage through the eye takes exactly 47 minutes)
- Constant pressure adjustments (atmospheric shear can tear airships apart)

Without the chronometer, these requirements become guesswork. With it repaired, they're achievable—but difficult.

Weather Officer Hastings predicts the storm is intensifying faster than normal. The safe passage window is shrinking.

Three hours until storm boundary. After that, the decision is made: passage or retreat.

The *Brass Phoenix* was built for this. The question is whether her crew—and her saboteur—will let her succeed.

## Hidden Truths

### The "Special Shipment" Contains Prototype Engine Technology

The four sealed crates in the cargo hold don't contain diplomatic correspondence or luxury goods. They contain experimental steam turbine prototypes worth 100,000 crowns—the result of three years of research by Meridian Airship Lines' engineering division.

These prototypes represent the next generation of dirigible propulsion: more efficient, more powerful, and capable of 15,000-foot operations. Whoever controls this technology will dominate the luxury airship market for the next decade.

Only four people aboard the *Brass Phoenix* know what's in the crates:
1. Captain Ironwood (shipping the prototypes to Meridian Station for trade summit demonstration)
2. Cargo Master Hendricks (loaded them personally, sworn to secrecy)
3. **Cordelia Vex** (industrial spy who learned through bribed port workers)
4. **Professor Minerva Cogsworth** (Cordelia's accomplice, pretending to be innocent inventor)

The chronometer sabotage was never about destroying the ship. It was about delaying arrival at Meridian Station past the trade summit deadline, causing Meridian Airship Lines to forfeit the Consolidated Steamworks contract—worth 2 million crowns over five years.

With the contract forfeited, Free Territories competitors (who employed Cordelia) would win by default. And in the chaos of a failed maiden voyage, the prototype crates could be "lost" and smuggled to competitor engineering teams.

---

### Professor Cogsworth Wrote the Blackmail Letter

The blackmail letter that coerced Chief Engineer Graylock was written by Professor Minerva Cogsworth, a brilliant engineer working undercover for Cordelia Vex and Free Territories intelligence.

Cogsworth has genuine engineering expertise—her "theories" about mechanical systems aren't fabrications. She was recruited by Cordelia specifically because her technical knowledge made her a credible passenger while giving her the ability to create convincing sabotage instructions.

The chronometer specifications in Graylock's blackmail letter match Cogsworth's precise drafting style. If Adelaide or an expert examiner analyzes the technical drawings closely (DC 20 Investigation check), they'll recognize the diagrams were drawn by someone with formal Academy training—eliminating Graylock as the author and pointing to an educated saboteur.

Cogsworth obtained the chronometer specifications three months ago by bribing a junior draftsman at the Phoenix's manufacturing company. The draftsman is now dead (officially a tavern brawl; actually murdered by Cordelia's operatives to eliminate witnesses).

---

### Cordelia Vex Runs the Underdock Syndicate's Gambling Operation

Cordelia Vex isn't just an industrial spy—she's the architect behind Coalport's illegal gambling network. The "Underdock Syndicate" that Graylock owes 8,000 crowns isn't a faceless criminal organization. It's Cordelia's operation, designed specifically to create leverage over vulnerable crew members.

She identified Graylock six months ago through port informants who reported his growing gambling habit. She rigged the games, inflated his debts, then used those debts to coerce him into sabotage.

Graylock was the perfect target:
- Chief engineer with access to navigation systems
- Financially desperate
- Not sophisticated enough to recognize manipulation
- Too ashamed to confess to anyone

The blackmail scheme worked perfectly—Graylock created the sabotage, disabled the chronometer, and kept silent. Even his confession to Adelaide and Silas didn't reveal Cordelia as the orchestrator, because Graylock genuinely doesn't know who sent the letter.

Lord Ashworth's gambling debts are also to Cordelia's operation (the "V.C." initials in Graylock's records match "Vex Cordelia" if anyone checks). Ashworth owes 3,000 crowns and will do favors to reduce the debt—like staying silent about suspicious passenger behavior.

---

### The Encrypted Aethergram Was a Progress Report

The encrypted aethergram sent from the communications deck at 02:17 hours was Professor Cogsworth reporting to Free Territories handlers:

> "Chronometer disabled. Crystal secured. Timeline intact. Arrival delay guaranteed. Prototypes accessible post-failure."

The message was transmitted using a one-time cipher that Cogsworth memorized before boarding. Even if intercepted and decoded (extremely difficult without the cipher key), it looks like commercial correspondence about shipping delays.

Cogsworth sent the message immediately after Graylock completed the sabotage, confirming the operation's success. She used the emergency override panel specifically because it bypasses normal logging—evidence of insider knowledge about ship's communication systems.

If the party recovers the aethergram transmission log and brings it to a codebreaker (available at Meridian Station), they could decode it within 2-3 days—but only if they suspect it's relevant to the sabotage.

## Latent Threats

### The Storm Window Is Actually 2 Hours, Not 3

Weather Officer Hastings is wrong. The safe navigation window through the Titanstorm isn't 3 hours—it's 2 hours.

Hastings is using outdated atmospheric prediction models that underestimate storm intensification rates. The Titanstorm's eye is shrinking faster than predicted, and wind shear at the boundaries is increasing to lethal levels.

Captain Ironwood suspects this but hasn't announced it to avoid panic. He's watching the barometric readings himself and will make the navigate-or-retreat decision at the 1-hour mark—earlier than anyone expects.

If the party wastes time on non-essential investigations, they'll suddenly face a crisis deadline when Ironwood announces: "Storm passage in 45 minutes or we abort the voyage."

---

### Cordelia Plans to Sabotage the Backup Chronometer Next

Cordelia knows the primary chronometer has been repaired. She's not deterred—she's already planning contingency sabotage.

If the party doesn't identify and detain her within the next 90 minutes, she'll disable the backup chronometer by "accidentally" spilling acid (from her "cosmetics case") on its mechanism during a passenger saloon "stumble."

With both chronometers disabled, navigating the Titanstorm becomes impossible. The Phoenix would be forced to abort, missing the trade summit deadline.

Cordelia is patient, careful, and willing to escalate. If direct sabotage is too risky, she'll create a passenger "incident" (fire, injury, panic) that forces Captain Ironwood to turn back for humanitarian reasons.

---

### The Emergency Gondola Has Been Tampered With

If the ship faces catastrophic failure and orders emergency evacuation, passengers would discover the emergency gondola's release mechanism has been jammed with a small brass wedge—matching the one used in the chronometer sabotage.

This is Cordelia's insurance: if the ship actually starts to fail (beyond her plan), she's ensured that evacuation will be delayed long enough for her to access the cargo hold and seize at least one prototype crate before escaping via hidden parachute in her cabin.

The gondola tampering won't be discovered unless someone specifically inspects the emergency equipment (unlikely during normal operations). But if disaster strikes and the gondola won't deploy, 36 people will be trapped on a dying airship.

---

### Professor Cogsworth Has Explosives

Professor Cogsworth's "mechanical theories" research includes a small quantity of nitroglycerine-based demolition compound hidden in a false-bottom case labeled "Chemical Analysis Supplies."

She brought it aboard as final contingency: if sabotage fails, if the ship successfully navigates the storm, if Cordelia's plans collapse—Cogsworth will plant explosives in the cargo hold to destroy the prototype engine crates.

Better to destroy the technology than let Meridian Airship Lines demonstrate it at the trade summit.

The explosives are stable but powerful enough to breach the hull if detonated in the cargo hold—killing everyone aboard. Cogsworth would prefer to avoid mass murder, but she's been told "mission success justifies any cost."

If confronted and cornered, she'll threaten to detonate the explosives unless granted safe passage.

## Unrevealed Actors

### Cordelia Vex (Industrial Spy & Master Orchestrator)

**True Identity:** Captain Cordelia Vex, Free Territories Industrial Intelligence Corps

**Cover Story:** Wealthy merchant traveling to Meridian Station for trade opportunities

**Real Mission:**
- Delay *Brass Phoenix* to force trade summit deadline failure
- Steal or destroy prototype engine technology
- Ensure Meridian Airship Lines loses Consolidated Steamworks contract

**Current Position:** Passenger saloon, maintaining innocent cover while monitoring investigation progress

**Resources:**
- Locked brass case (contains cipher key, forged documents, backup identity papers)
- 8,000 crowns in bearer bonds (for bribes/escape)
- Hidden garrote wire (sewn into dress hem, for emergency eliminations)
- Network of paid informants at Coalport docks

**Weakness:** Overconfidence. Cordelia believes she's smarter than everyone aboard and will underestimate Adelaide's engineering insight and Silas's investigative persistence.

**If Discovered:**
Cordelia will attempt to:
1. Bluff and intimidate (claim diplomatic immunity as Free Territories citizen)
2. Bribe her way to freedom (offer Adelaide/Silas 2,000 crowns each to look the other way)
3. Threaten to expose Graylock's gambling debts publicly unless released
4. If all else fails, attempt escape via hidden parachute in her cabin

---

### Professor Minerva Cogsworth (Technical Specialist & Accomplice)

**True Identity:** Dr. Minerva Cogsworth, industrial sabotage specialist, Academy-trained engineer

**Cover Story:** Independent inventor researching mechanical propulsion theories

**Real Mission:**
- Provide technical expertise for chronometer sabotage
- Write blackmail letter with authentic engineering specifications
- Serve as Cordelia's backup if primary sabotage fails
- Destroy prototype engines if sabotage is exposed

**Current Position:** Passenger cabin, working on "research" (actually preparing explosive contingency)

**Resources:**
- Demolition explosives (hidden in false-bottom case)
- Advanced engineering knowledge (formal Academy training)
- Backup sabotage plans (written in cipher in her notebook)
- Remote detonator (disguised as pocket watch)

**Weakness:** Genuine conscience. Cogsworth is technically brilliant but morally conflicted. She doesn't want to kill innocent people—she's been told the mission is "industrial competition, not mass murder." If confronted with evidence that her actions will kill civilians, she might break.

**If Discovered:**
Cogsworth will:
1. Deny involvement (claim she's just a researcher)
2. If pressed, admit to writing the letter but claim Cordelia coerced her
3. If cornered, threaten to detonate explosives in cargo hold
4. If shown mercy/offered immunity, might betray Cordelia to save herself

- That the sabotage is connected to the cargo

## GM Notes

### The Real Stakes

This isn't just about solving a sabotage case. It's about:

1. **Industrial espionage** - 2 million crown contract and prototype technology
2. **Personal redemption** - Adelaide proving her competence, Silas showing he's more than working-class security
3. **Class conflict** - Working-class crew vs. nobility passengers, who's really responsible?
4. **Moral complexity** - Graylock is victim and perpetrator; Professor Cogsworth is brilliant but compromised
5. **Time pressure** - Storm window closing creates escalating tension

### Multiple Victory Conditions

**Complete Success:**
- Identify Cordelia and Cogsworth
- Secure confessions/evidence
- Navigate storm successfully
- Arrive on time
- Protect prototypes

**Partial Success:**
- Navigate storm successfully but saboteurs escape
- OR catch saboteurs but miss deadline
- OR survive but with casualties/damage

**Failure:**
- Ship destroyed in storm
- Saboteurs escape with prototypes
- Adelaide/Silas blamed for disaster
- Meridian Airship Lines ruined

### NPCs Working at Cross-Purposes

- **Graylock** wants redemption through confession
- **Cordelia** wants mission success through continued deception
- **Cogsworth** wants mission success but not mass murder
- **Ashworth** wants survival through cooperation
- **Captain Ironwood** wants maiden voyage success regardless of cost
- **Hendricks** wants to follow orders and protect cargo

The party must navigate these conflicting interests to achieve optimal outcome.

### Recommended Revelation Sequence

**Hour 1:** Party investigates, discovers Graylock's involvement, recovers crystal

**Hour 2:** Party finds blackmail letter, analyzes technical drawings, connects to Cogsworth

**Hour 3:** Realization that sabotage is industrial espionage targeting prototypes

**Final Hour:** Confront Cordelia and Cogsworth before/during storm passage

This pacing builds tension as the storm deadline approaches while revelations cascade.

### If Party Gets Stuck

**Hints to Deploy:**
1. Captain Ironwood mentions trade summit importance casually
2. Cargo Master Hendricks nervously guards "special shipment"
3. Adelaide notices Cogsworth's technical diagram style matches blackmail letter
4. Silas spots Cordelia and Ashworth in hushed conversation
5. Weather Officer announces storm intensifying faster than predicted

### The Ultimate Question

Can the party:
- Solve the conspiracy
- Navigate the storm
- Protect the cargo
- Achieve justice

…all before the chronometer runs out?

The *Brass Phoenix* can fly through any storm—if her crew can overcome sabotage, espionage, and class conflict before time expires.

The chronometer is ticking. The storm is waiting. And 36 souls depend on the party getting this right.
